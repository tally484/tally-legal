privacy policy

hexis — habit journal · effective 10 september 2026

hexis is built to be private. the short version: your journal stays with you, and we never see it. the only times anything from it leaves your device are ones you choose — iCloud sync, a group you start or join, a backup you export, or a card you share — and even then it goes to Apple or to wherever you sent it, never to us. one thing does leave without your choosing it, and only one: the shop asks a service called RevenueCat whether you have paid. it is told nothing about your habits. what it is told is set out below.

hexis was called tally until august 2026. same app, same developer, same policy — this page moved with it, which is why its address still says tally.

what we collect

nothing about you, and nothing out of your journal. hexis has no analytics, no tracking, no advertising, no crash reporting, and no hexis account — there is no hexis server anywhere, so there is nowhere for your habits to go and no one here to read them.

hexis carries one piece of third-party code: RevenueCat, which is how the app knows whether you have bought hexis+. it is the only part of hexis that speaks to anyone other than Apple, it runs only around a purchase, and no habit, mark, signature or contract is ever passed to it. what it does receive is under purchases.

where your data lives

your habits, their marks, streaks and settings are stored in a file on your device. what that file holds is the list of habits you made — each one's name, icon, target, cadence, reminder time, and which days you marked kept, rested, or mended — plus your signatures and any hexis contracts you signed. nothing else about you is in it.

hexis also offers optional iCloud sync, off by default. when you turn it on in settings, that file is mirrored to your private iCloud, encrypted in transit and at rest and reachable only by your Apple ID — we have no way to read it. turning sync off stops further mirroring.

deleting the app removes the copy on your device. if iCloud sync was ever on, the mirrored copy stays in your own iCloud until you remove it there — iOS Settings › your name › iCloud › Manage Account Storage. we cannot remove it for you, and we cannot see it either.

your Apple Watch

if you install the watch app, hexis keeps a copy of your journal on the watch so you can mark a day without your phone. it carries your habit names, icons, cadences, contracts and which days are marked — enough to draw the same journal — and it travels straight between your two devices over Apple's device-to-device link. it does not pass through a server, and it does not pass through us.

your signature does not go to the watch. deleting the watch app removes the watch's copy.

widgets and complications

the home screen widgets and the watch face complication read the same on-device journal and draw from it. they send nothing anywhere.

your signature

when you sign a commitment, hexis keeps what you drew as a set of points, on your device, alongside the habit you signed for. it is mirrored to your private iCloud if you have sync on. it is never shown to anyone else — not to a group, not to the watch, not to us.

groups

a group lets a few people keep one habit side by side. it is entirely optional — nothing is shared until you start a group or accept an invite, and the rest of the app works the same either way.

a group lives in a shared iCloud area hosted by the person who created it. Apple carries it; there is no hexis server, and we cannot read a group's contents.

what the other members of a group can see:

  • the name you type for yourself when you join, the colour you are drawn in, and your device's time zone;
  • for the one habit you point at that group, which days you marked kept, rested, or mended — the dates only;
  • if you created the group: the group's name, plus the name, icon, and weekly cadence of the habit you built it around.

what never leaves your device: every other habit in your journal, and everything private about the habit you did share — its amounts, its reminders, its contracts, and your signature. a group carries dates and a display name, nothing more.

how you are identified in a group. Apple issues hexis an identifier for your iCloud account, scoped to this app. that is what marks your row as yours. it is not your Apple ID, your email, or your real name, and we never see it — but it is the same identifier in every group you share with someone, which is what lets blocking follow a person from one group to another.

the invite is a link, and anyone who has it can join and see the group. share it only with people you mean to let in. the app says this where the invite is created.

you can leave a group at any time; your member record is removed from it and your own marks stay in your journal, untouched. the person who created a group hosts it — if they end it, or delete the app, the group ends for everyone in it.

if you report someone, the report is composed on your device as an email to the address at the foot of this page, and you send it. it names the group, the member, and whatever you choose to write. we read it and act on it; it goes nowhere else.

backups you export

exporting a backup writes your whole journal — every habit, every mark, your signatures and contracts — into a single file that you then save or send wherever you choose. from that moment the file is yours to look after: it is no longer inside the app's protection, and we have no part in where it goes.

sharing a card

when you share your progress, the image is drawn on your device and handed to whatever you share it to. what happens next is that app's business, under its rules rather than ours. hexis copies to your clipboard and shares out; it never reads your clipboard.

purchases

the money is Apple's business, not ours. subscriptions, any free trial, and the older one-time unlock are all handled through the App Store, on your App Store account. we never see your payment details — no card, no billing address, nothing of the sort ever reaches us or anyone acting for us.

to work out what to unlock, hexis uses RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., United States), which reads Apple's purchase receipt and answers one question: does this device have hexis+? for that it is sent the App Store receipt and purchase history for this app — which plan, when it was bought, whether it is in a trial, when it renews or lapsed — together with the device's platform, operating system, app version, language and country, and the IP address the request arrives on, which it uses to place the purchase in a country and not to locate you.

it is not given a name, an email address, your Apple ID, or an advertising identifier. hexis has no accounts, so there is no name to give: the app asks RevenueCat to issue an anonymous identifier instead of supplying one, and the record it keeps is that random string. RevenueCat can be asked by an app to collect device and advertising identifiers for ad attribution — hexis never asks, and runs no attribution or marketing tracking of any kind. RevenueCat handles this on our behalf, under its own privacy policy; we use it to see whether a purchase is live, and it is not sold or handed on for advertising.

the hexis+ page itself is fetched from RevenueCat too, so its wording and its prices can be corrected without shipping a new version of the app.

if RevenueCat can't be reached, nothing you paid for is lost: hexis also checks Apple's receipt on the device itself, and takes either answer as a yes — so a phone that has not been online since you bought hexis+ still has it.

you can manage or cancel a subscription in your App Store account settings.

notifications

habit reminders are local notifications, scheduled and delivered on your device. they name the habit they are for, so they are as private as your lock screen is. they never touch a server, and hexis has no push notifications of any kind.

children

hexis does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children — because it collects no personal information at all.

changes

if this policy ever changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date. the previous version of this page said hexis contained no third-party code at all; that was true until september 2026, when RevenueCat was added to handle purchases, and this version says so.
