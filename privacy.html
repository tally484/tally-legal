Privacy Policy

Hexis · Updated 14 September 2026

Features depend on the version of Hexis installed on your device. The Groups and Reporting sections apply only if your app offers a Groups tab. Without that feature, Hexis does not create or join shared groups. Other sections describe features where available. How earlier TestFlight builds handled Groups is described in the earlier policy and in Groups in earlier builds below. The app was previously called Tally.

Overview

Hexis is a private habit journal developed by Martin Elenjikkal. There is no Hexis account or developer-operated journal server. No advertising, tracking or remote analytics SDK is included. The app does not automatically send your journal to the developer. Information can move through optional private iCloud sync, your paired Apple Watch, exports and sharing you initiate, and emails you choose to send.

Your journal and private iCloud

Habit names, icons, targets, amounts, cadence, reminders, daily marks, streaks, routines, settings, signatures and commitments are stored on your devices. Optional iCloud sync is off by default. It mirrors habits, signatures, earned-mend balance and change/deletion records to your private iCloud using Apple's key-value store. Routines are not included. Turning sync off stops further mirroring; the developer cannot read or restore your private iCloud data.

Removing the app does not erase existing iCloud data or exported backups. Manage iCloud storage through your Apple Account settings, and contact Apple Support if you need help with retained Apple-hosted data. Keep a backup before deleting storage you may need.

Apple Watch, widgets and reminders

The paired Watch receives a habit snapshot over Apple's device-to-device connection and sends habit marks back. The current snapshot includes signature data and other habit fields that the Watch does not display. Routines are not sent to the Watch. Widgets and complications use on-device journal storage; marks made on these surfaces can subsequently sync to your own devices.

Habit and routine reminders are local notifications. Routine Live Activities are driven by local state. The app registers for remote notifications so Apple can tell it silently that a group has changed; see Groups. No developer push server is used, and no notification carries journal content.

Backups and sharing

Exported backups contain habits, daily marks, amounts, routines, mend balance, signatures and commitments. Protect these files. Sending a backup or screenshot to someone gives that recipient its contents. Progress cards are generated locally and handed to the app or destination you choose, whose privacy rules then apply. There is no in-app social feed, public profile or discoverable content; the one way journal information reaches another person inside Hexis is a group you create or accept an invitation to, described next.

Groups

Groups is optional and inactive unless you create or accept an invitation to one. Each participant needs a Hexis+ entitlement and an iCloud account; an eligible earlier purchase can provide the entitlement without a current subscription. A group is hosted in its creator's own private iCloud, as a shared record zone. There is no developer-operated group server, and the developer cannot read, change or delete what is in a group — a creator's private iCloud database is not reachable by us.

Invitations are private. Invitations are issued to specific people through Apple's own sharing controls. Public link access is not enabled, so holding a forwarded invitation does not by itself allow an uninvited Apple Account to join.

What the other members of a group can see. The group's name; the name, icon and weekly cadence of the single habit the group is built around; and, for each member, the display name they joined under, a colour index, their time zone, when they joined, when they last published, and their marks for that one habit — completed days, intentional rest days, days covered by a mend, and dated edit receipts retained for about 90 days. Nothing else from your journal is put into a group: your other habits, routines, amount targets, reminders, signatures, commitments and history stay out of it. Your own copy of the habit may be named differently in your private journal from the name the group displays.

On your device. A copy of the group is cached on your device so its page works offline, and Apple's silent notifications tell the app when something has changed. Which of your habits is connected to which group is recorded only on your device and is never shared.

Names are checked on your device before they are published. A group's name, the name you show up under, and the name of the habit a group is built around are compared against a list held inside the app, and refused if they match. This check runs entirely on your device and transmits nothing.

Leaving, removing, ending and blocking do different things.

  • Leave removes your member record, and the marks it carried, from that group, and stops your marks being published to it.
  • Manage Access, available to the group's creator through Apple's sharing screen, can remove a person's access so they can no longer see or add to the group. It does not by itself delete what they have already written.
  • End group, available to the creator, deletes the shared zone: the group stops existing for everyone in it.
  • Block hides that person's row on your device, in every group you share with them, immediately and reversibly. It is a local control: it does not delete their information, does not revoke their access to the group, and does not notify them.
  • Deleting the app or signing out of iCloud does not by itself delete the creator's shared CloudKit zone or revoke invitations. End the group explicitly before uninstalling if that is what you intend.

Reporting someone in a group

Each member's row offers Report. The app sends the report over an encrypted connection to a report intake operated by the developer, which records it in a dedicated store separate from the group and from your private journal. This intake is the one part of Hexis where something of the developer's sits between your device and your information; groups themselves still have nothing of ours in them. The app does not put a report in the group or notify the person reported.

Who can read a report. Only the developer. The app cannot read, change or delete reports, and neither can anyone else using Hexis — no copy of the app holds access to them. Only a key held by the intake can write one, and that key is not in the app. In builds before this one the app wrote reports itself, which required a permission that would also have let other signed-in users read them; no report from any user was ever stored under that arrangement.

The intake runs on Cloudflare, which processes the request for us and may keep short-term operational logs, as any web service does. Like any web request, it shows the network address yours comes from; that address is used to limit how many reports can be sent at once, and is not written into the report or stored with it.

A report contains a reference code, the reason you selected, any note you write, the reported member's display name and their group member identifier, the group's name and identifier, your own group member identifier when available, the app version, and the time. It contains no habit marks or other journal fields unless you type them into the note.

The app first tries to save a report on your device, then sends it. If sending fails but local saving succeeds, the app says it is waiting and retries when Groups refreshes. The device holds up to twenty waiting reports. If it cannot save another report, it still tries to send; if that also fails, it offers an email draft or a copy for you to send, without discarding reports already waiting. Sending email discloses your email address to us, as any email does. Reports do not expire on their own; they remain until the developer deletes them. Reports are used for safety and support, not advertising. Quote the report's reference code to request access or deletion; information needed for an unresolved safety or legal matter may need to be retained. Blocking takes effect on your device whether or not a report is delivered.

Groups in earlier builds, and retained information

Changes to the app do not automatically delete shared iCloud records created by an earlier version, revoke invitations already accepted, or alter copies of the app already installed on other people's devices.

Groups in earlier TestFlight builds could be shared by a public invitation link, which allowed anyone holding the link to join. Those older shares are not converted merely by installing a later version. If you created a group in a TestFlight build, open it and use Apple's Manage Access screen to check who has access and how it is shared, and end the group if you no longer want it. The same categories of information listed above were shared in those builds; the earlier policy describes them.

The developer cannot erase another person's private iCloud zone, and will not claim information has been deleted without confirming it. Contact us, or Apple Support, for help with data held in iCloud.

Purchases

Apple processes purchases, trials, subscriptions and refunds through your App Store account. Hexis uses StoreKit to load products and verify entitlements. Purchases and restores communicate with Apple; valid local entitlement records can support offline access. The app does not send journal contents with purchase checks, and the developer does not receive card details. Removing a feature or uninstalling the app does not cancel a subscription; manage it in your Apple Account subscription settings.

Local diagnostics

A small first-run event log is stored on your device, not transmitted. It records events such as onboarding and paywall presentation, with timestamps, but no habit names or daily marks. It is not connected to a remote analytics service.

Support, suggestions and earlier reports

Support and suggestion controls prepare emails for you to review and send from your own mail app. Opening or discarding a draft does not send it. We receive your email address, displayed sender name, message and any attachments you include. In-app messages may include the app version; suggestions also include their selected category and preparation time. They do not automatically attach your journal.

Reports about a group member are described under Reporting someone in a group. We use correspondence for support, resolving reported problems and safety, not advertising or marketing. It is held in the developer's email account for as long as needed for those purposes. Contact us to request access or deletion; limited records may need to be retained for legal or unresolved safety obligations.

Children

Hexis is not directed at children and does not knowingly solicit their personal information. If a child sends personal information to support, a parent or guardian can contact us to request deletion. A group is the one part of Hexis where something a person types is shown to other people; it can only be entered by accepting an invitation from someone who already has it, and a parent or guardian who wants a child removed from one should use Leave in the app, ask the group's creator to remove their access, and may contact us for help.

Changes and contact

This revision distinguishes features by installed version, describes the invitation-only form of new Groups and the separate reporting route, and does not claim that information shared by earlier TestFlight builds has disappeared. It also records that reports now go to a developer-operated intake rather than being written by the app, and who can read them. Future material changes will be reflected here.
